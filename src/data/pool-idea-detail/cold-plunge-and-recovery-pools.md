Cold-water immersion (CWI) has moved from locker-room folklore to a mainstream recovery routine. But a dedicated plunge needs more than a stock tank: walls rigid enough to brace against, insulation to slow warming, a cover to keep debris out between sessions, and a footprint that fits a garage gym or a shaded patio corner. This guide covers how to build that setup with drop-stitch tubs — and where the honest limits are.

## 1. Why Drop-Stitch Works for Cold Plunge

A plunge vessel gets used hard: you grip the rim, brace against the wall, and step in and out daily. Soft air-ring pools flex under that treatment. A [drop-stitch tub](/guides/what-is-a-drop-stitch-inflatable-pool) inflated to its rated 4–5 PSI operating pressure holds a firm, straight-walled shape with a stable rim for support during entry and exit.

Inflatable Pool Factory SPA tubs add the two features a plunge actually needs: **built-in seats** for an upright seated immersion position, and a **5 mm insulated thermal bottom** that slows heat transfer from the ground — useful whether you are keeping heat out (cold plunge) or in (hot soak).

## 2. Cooling Strategy: Ice or Active Chilling

An honest point first: **insulation slows temperature change, it does not create it.** To hold plunge temperatures (roughly 4–10 °C / 39–50 °F) in warm environments you need one of two approaches:

- **Ice-based:** add bagged ice and replace as it melts. Simple and equipment-free, but requires disciplined water changes every 3–7 days since there is no sanitizer loop running.
- **Active chilling:** pair the tub with an external water chiller through the standard circulation ports. Confirm port compatibility with your chiller vendor before purchasing — see the specs in the [shop](/products).

A fitted, insulated cover is non-negotiable for either route: it locks in temperature, blocks debris, keeps energy use down, and keeps children and pets out of an unsupervised water vessel.

## 4. Water Hygiene for Daily Immersion

Daily single-user plunges are low-contamination, but small volumes still need a routine:

- **Skim and cover after every session.** The cover is your primary hygiene tool.
- **For chilled, filtered setups:** run the circulation and sanitizer system per the equipment vendor's guidance, and test the water regularly.
- **For ice-based setups:** drain and refill on a 3–7 day cycle depending on use and ambient temperature — small volumes make this fast with the hose-compatible drain valve.
- **Rinse before entering** when you are coming straight from a workout; sweat is the main organic load.

## 5. Health & Safety Disclaimers

> **Read before starting a cold-immersion routine.**

- **Consult a physician first** if you have high blood pressure, a heart condition, circulatory issues, are pregnant, or take medication that affects circulation or causes drowsiness. Cold immersion places real physiological stress on the cardiovascular system.
- **Never plunge alone or while impaired.** Have someone within earshot, keep sessions short at first (around 2–5 minutes), and exit immediately if you feel dizzy or unwell.
- **Not a medical device.** Inflatable Pool Factory tubs are water vessels. They are not sold to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition, and no health outcome is guaranteed.
- **Supervise the vessel.** A filled tub of any temperature is a drowning hazard for children — keep it covered and inaccessible when not in use.
